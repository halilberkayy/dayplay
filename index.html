The alarm you can't snooze away

Turning the alarm off is easy. Getting up is hard. DayPlay rings a real alarm through Silent mode and Focus, and with mission lock it does not stop until you confirm the first step of your routine.

What makes it different

  • A real alarm, not a notification. Built on AlarmKit, Apple's system alarm framework (iOS 26 and later), so it breaks through Silent mode, Focus and Do Not Disturb.
  • Mission lock. The stop button does not send you back to bed, it opens your routine. The alarm keeps ringing until you confirm step one.
  • Counted snoozes. You decide how many snoozes you get. When they run out the snooze button is gone, not greyed out.
  • Escape protection. Force quitting the app while the mission is locked re arms the alarm instead of ending it.
  • Full screen player. After you are up, one step fills the screen with a big countdown and a single button.
  • Honest timing. The summary shows planned versus actual minutes with no flattery.
  • Lock Screen and Dynamic Island. The countdown stays in view, and there is an Apple Watch companion.

Who it is for

People who turn off three alarms in their sleep. People with ADHD, night shifts, or a morning routine that falls apart between the alarm and the first task. DayPlay is not a medical treatment; it is a tool designed around how an unreliable morning actually fails.

Questions people ask

Is there an iPhone alarm app that rings when the phone is on silent?

Yes. Since iOS 26, apps can use AlarmKit, Apple's system alarm framework, and DayPlay does. Its alarm rings through Silent mode, Focus and Do Not Disturb, with a full screen alert on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island. On iOS 18 and earlier DayPlay sends a burst of notifications instead, which Silent mode can mute, and the app says so on screen.

How does DayPlay get heavy sleepers out of bed?

Three ways. Mission lock turns the stop button into the first step of your routine, and the alarm keeps ringing until you confirm that step. A morning proof photo can stand in for it: you photograph a spot you walk to after waking, like the sink, and the alarm stops when a new photo matches it (if the camera fails, confirming the first step works too). Snoozes are counted, zero to three per routine, and when they run out the snooze button disappears.

What happens if I close the app to stop the alarm?

While mission lock is on, DayPlay keeps a backup system alarm about 30 seconds ahead. Leaving the app before step one is confirmed pulls it to about 3 seconds, so the alarm comes back instead of ending.

Is DayPlay an ADHD app?

It is built to be ADHD friendly: one step on screen at a time, a big countdown, no shaming language, and planned versus actual minutes side by side so your estimates get better. It is not a medical treatment and does not diagnose or treat ADHD.

How is DayPlay different from the iPhone Clock app?

Both ring through Silent mode (DayPlay on iOS 26 and later). The Clock app stops with one tap and lets you snooze without limit. DayPlay can require the first step of your routine or a matching photo before it stops, caps snoozes at a number you choose, and then plays your routine step by step with a timer.

No account. Alarms, routines and the player work offline, and your routines and history stay on the phone. A connection is used for purchases and, if you leave them on, usage counters and crash reports; both can be turned off in Settings, Privacy.
